Locations

Locations are the places you look after, set out the way you think about them. A location can be inside another location, as many levels deep as you need. For example:

  • Head Office, a site
  • Head Office > Main Building, a building at that site
  • Head Office > Main Building > Ground Floor, a floor of that building

Each location's path is the names from the top down, separated by >. Paths are shown wherever a location is, so you can always tell which Ground Floor is meant.

Every asset, job and job schedule is at one location.

Adding locations

Go to Locations and choose Create. Leave Parent Location blank for a top-level location such as a site, or choose the location the new one is inside. You can also open a location and choose Add Sub-Location, which starts the new location inside it.

A location can also have a Main Contact: someone in your organisation to contact about it, chosen from your People. It's shown on the location's page. If that person is deleted, the location is left with no main contact.

The Locations page shows every location as a tree, with each one nested under the location it's in. Search it to find locations by name or path.

Categories

Because locations can nest in any way, you can give each one a Location Category to say what kind of place it is, such as Site, Building, Floor or Room. Add your categories under Reference data, then choose one when you create or edit a location. The Locations page shows each location's category in the tree, and its Category filter lists every location of one kind, for example all your buildings.

Names

  • Two locations can't have the same path. Two buildings at different sites can both be called "Main Building", but a site can't have two.
  • Paths are compared ignoring capitals, so "Head Office > North Block" and "head office > north block" count as the same.
  • A name can't contain >, because that separates the names in a path.

Renaming and moving

Edit a location to rename it or choose a different parent. Everything inside it moves with it: the paths of the locations inside it are updated, and their assets, jobs and job schedules stay where they are. A location can't be moved inside itself or inside one of its own sub-locations.

Choosing a location

When you create or edit an asset, job or job schedule, choose its Location from the list. Type part of a path to find it quickly. A job raised from a job schedule is given the schedule's location, and a sub-asset or follow-up job starts at the same location as the one it's created from.

Finding things by location

A location's page has tabs for its Jobs, Job Schedules, Assets and Files. The Jobs, Job Schedules and Assets tabs include the locations inside it, so a site's tabs show everything at the site.

Each of those tabs has a Create button, which adds a job, job schedule or asset at that location without choosing it again. In the same way, an asset's Jobs and Job Schedules tabs have a Create button next to Link, which creates one at the asset's location already linked to the asset. Either way you come back to the tab afterwards.

The Assets, Jobs and Job Schedules lists can be filtered by location, and the filter includes the locations inside the one you choose. Lists and downloads have a Location column showing the path.

When you link assets to a job or job schedule, the list offers assets anywhere in the same top-level location (site).

Deleting

Deleting a location deletes every location inside it, and all the assets, jobs and job schedules at those locations. The delete page shows how many of each will go before you confirm. Files linked to the locations aren't deleted, only unlinked.

Importing

Locations can be imported from an Excel or CSV file under Import data. Each row has a name, an optional location_category (one of your Location Categories), an optional main_contact (someone in your People: their full name, or their name with their email in brackets, such as Sam Jones (sam@example.com), when two people share a name) and, for a location inside another, a parent: the parent's full path, such as Head Office > Main Building. The parent can be an existing location or one added by an earlier row in the same file, so put each location after its parent.

Asset, job and job schedule imports have a location column, which is the location's full path. Capitals and the spaces around > don't matter. Import your locations first. The Excel template on each import page has a dropdown of your locations' paths in this column (and in a locations import's parent column), so you can pick one rather than type it. The list is your locations when you download the template; a location added since, or earlier in the same file, can still be typed in.

An import of locations can't be undone once any of them have assets, jobs or job schedules, or once other locations have been added inside them.

Permissions

Locations have their own row in the user group permissions, with view, create, edit and delete like the other records.
