Job Schedules

A job schedule is planned maintenance that repeats, such as an annual boiler service or a monthly emergency lighting test. The schedule raises a job each time the work is due. Each job is a planned job, with the schedule's description, work required, site, building and zone, statutory compliance and linked assets. See Sites, Buildings and Zones for how locations work.

Setting up a schedule

When you create a job schedule you give it:

  • Next Due: the date the next job is due.
  • Frequency: how often the work repeats.
  • Notice Days: how many days before the due date the job is raised, so there's time to arrange the work. This can be from 0 to 365, and no longer than the shortest gap between two jobs for the frequency. For example, a weekly schedule can have at most 7 days' notice.

The Next Job Creation date is worked out for you: the next due date minus the notice days. You can't set it directly.

Daily and weekly frequencies move on by an exact number of days. Monthly and yearly frequencies move on by calendar months, so a date on the 31st becomes the last day of a shorter month (for example 31 January plus one month is 28 February, or 29 February in a leap year).

How jobs are raised

Every day, MyFmHub checks all job schedules. A schedule whose Next Job Creation date is today, or has already passed, has a new job raised, due on the schedule's Next Due date.

The Next Job Creation date can already be in the past when you save a schedule, if the due date is closer than the notice days. The job is then raised the next time schedules are checked.

A schedule only has one job open at a time. While that job is open, the schedule's Next Due date stays the same and its Next Job Creation shows Pending current job closure. No more jobs are raised from it until the job is closed.

Closing a job from a schedule

Closing a planned job takes two steps, and the job is only closed once both are done:

  1. Enter the date the job was closed. It can't be in the future, or before the date the job was raised. This applies to every job, not just planned ones.
  2. Set when the next job is due. MyFmHub suggests the close date plus the schedule's frequency, and you can bring this date forward if needed. When you confirm, the job is closed and the schedule's Next Due and Next Job Creation dates are set.

The next due date:

  • can't be later than the suggested date, because the gap to the next job would then be longer than the schedule's frequency
  • can't be in the past
  • can only be today if the suggested date has already passed, for example when a weekly job is closed with a close date a month ago

If you leave the page before confirming, nothing is saved and the job stays open. Once the job is closed you can change the schedule's dates by editing the schedule.

Editing a schedule

A job schedule can't be edited while it has an open job. Close the job first, then edit the schedule.

This makes sure a schedule never has more than one open job. Editing the dates while a job is open could raise a second job for the same schedule, and the jobs would no longer follow each other in sequence: each job's close date sets when the next one is due, so two jobs open at once would break that chain.

Active and inactive schedules

Every schedule is Active unless you untick Active when creating or editing it. An inactive schedule stays set up with all its details, but it doesn't raise any jobs: the daily check skips it, and its Next Job Creation shows Inactive. You can use this to set up a schedule before the work starts, or to pause one.

To make an inactive schedule active again, edit it and tick Active. Its Next Due date must be in the future (tomorrow or later) when you do, so it doesn't immediately raise a job that's already due. Next Job Creation is then worked out from the new date in the usual way.

Making a schedule inactive is an edit, so it can't be done while the schedule has an open job. Close the job first.

Rules for planned jobs

A job raised from a schedule is a planned job. Each one's close date sets when the next is due, so these rules keep the jobs in sequence:

  • The due date can't be changed. It comes from the schedule. You can still edit the job's other details.
  • An open planned job can't be deleted. The schedule is waiting for that job to close before it raises the next one, so deleting it would stop the schedule. Close it instead. Once it's closed it can be deleted.
  • A closed planned job can't be reopened. Closing it set the schedule's next due date, so reopening it would leave the schedule with two open jobs. If more work is needed, create a new job.

Deleting a schedule

A job schedule can only be deleted if it has never raised a job. Once it has, it's kept so its jobs stay linked to it and the history of the sequence isn't lost. Deleting a site still deletes everything at that site, including its schedules and their jobs.

Example

A monthly schedule is next due on 1 December with 7 notice days, so its Next Job Creation date is 24 November.

  • On 24 November a job is raised, due 1 December. The schedule's Next Job Creation shows Pending current job closure.
  • The job is closed with a close date of 28 November. MyFmHub suggests 28 December as the next due date.
  • You accept 28 December. The job is closed, and the schedule is next due on 28 December, with its next job raised on 21 December.
